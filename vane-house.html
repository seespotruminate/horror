THE HOUSE EXHALES

The name leaves your lips — Aldous Vane — and the circle answers.
Below the floor, four people open their eyes. Eleanor. Margaret. Thomas. And the master, who finally sleeps where he should.
The dark comes apart like wet paper. The candles burn blue, then out.

When your sight returns, you are standing in the morning light, on the road.
Behind you, Vane House is already a wall of ivy and ruin, as if it never stood.

In your coat: a bone flute, warm as a living thing.

The secret of the house was never the haunting.
It was a house that fed on the grief of those who loved it —
and kept the family as its heartbeat.
You set it free.
